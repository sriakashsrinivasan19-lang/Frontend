Lesson 01 • Foundational Principles

What is Artificial Intelligence?

In rigorous computational terms, Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the engineering of systems that formulate representations of environments, learn mathematical decision boundaries from observational data, and execute autonomous tasks.

1. Empirical Learning

Rather than depending on static hardcoded branching rules, models extract generalizable statistical weights directly from datasets.

2. Pattern Extraction

Algorithms map high-dimensional vectors across massive corpora to discover non-linear relationships invisible to human inspection.

3. Inference & Actuation

Once optimized, the model performs real-time probabilistic inference: generating tokens, segmenting image boundaries, or steering robotics.

Lesson 02 • Systematic Taxonomy

Types of Artificial Intelligence

Computer scientists categorize AI systems by evolutionary cognitive capability and functional architecture.

Active

Narrow AI (ANI)

Artificial Narrow Intelligence

Algorithms optimized to execute dedicated single-domain tasks with high precision, possessing zero transfer capability outside bounded boundaries.

Real World: Siri, AlphaFold, Tesla Autopilot, Midjourney.
Active Research R&D

General AI (AGI)

Artificial General Intelligence

Theoretical computational architectures possessing human-level cognitive flexibility across all abstract intellectual and reasoning domains.

Status: Active benchmark target pursued by frontier labs.
Theoretical Future

Super AI (ASI)

Artificial Superintelligence

Hypothetical autonomous intelligence orders of magnitude superior to all biological human intellect combined.

Status: Theoretical singularity hypothesis.
